01How I Think

My product brain.

I don't start with features. I start with why the problem exists — then work the mess until it becomes something a team can actually ship.

01

Find the real problem

The stated problem is rarely the actual one. I dig until the "why" stops moving.

02

Connect the dots

Business goal → user behaviour → product → technology → outcome. In that order, every time.

03

Turn ambiguity into action

Messy requirements don't scare me. They're usually where the interesting work begins.

04

Build with AI

I look for where AI changes the workflow itself — not where to bolt on a chatbot.

05

Think in outcomes

Shipping isn't the finish line. Impact is. One metric, one named owner.

06Unpopular Opinions

Things I'll say out loud.

01

A roadmap is not a strategy.

+

A roadmap is a sequence of bets. Strategy is knowing which bets you're refusing to make, and why. If your roadmap survives every new piece of information unchanged, it isn't a strategy — it's a queue.

02

More features rarely fix a broken product.

+

Feature requests are symptoms. When a product is broken, adding features is how you bury the problem under new surface area. I'd rather delete three things than ship a fourth that compensates for them.

03

Engineers should be in the room before the solution exists.

+

I was an engineer. The best technical ideas come from people who saw the raw problem — not a finished PRD. Handing engineers a solution to build wastes the smartest people in the building. I even built a game about this.

04

AI isn't a feature.

+

Adding a chatbot because everyone else did is decoration. The real question is whether AI can collapse an entire workflow — remove the step, not decorate it. If it can't, don't ship it.

05

A beautiful prototype can hide a terrible decision.

+

Polish is persuasive — that's what makes it dangerous. I stress-test the decision underneath before falling for the Figma. Shipping isn't the outcome; the metric moving is.
